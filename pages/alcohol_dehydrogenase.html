ENZYMEBASE
Alcohol Dehydrogenase (EC 1.1.1.1)

Accepted Name

Alcohol Dehydrogenase

Other Names

ADH, ethanol dehydrogenase, NAD-dependent alcohol dehydrogenase

Comments

Alcohol dehydrogenase is a key enzyme involved in the metabolism of alcohols in many organisms. It catalyzes the reversible oxidation of alcohols to aldehydes or ketones using NAD⁺ as a cofactor. It plays an important role in ethanol metabolism in the liver.

Function and Importance

This enzyme facilitates the breakdown of ethanol to acetaldehyde, which is further metabolized to acetic acid. It is crucial for detoxification of alcohol in the human body. Variations in ADH isoforms affect alcohol tolerance and susceptibility to alcoholism.

  • Central in ethanol metabolism and detoxification.
  • Involved in metabolism of various primary and secondary alcohols.
  • Multiple isoenzymes with tissue-specific expression.
  • Genetic polymorphisms influence enzyme activity and alcohol sensitivity.

Structure Details

Alcohol dehydrogenase is a zinc-containing enzyme typically existing as a dimer or tetramer. It contains a catalytic zinc ion essential for its activity and a structural zinc ion that stabilizes the protein structure. The enzyme binds NAD⁺ and the alcohol substrate in its active site to perform the oxidation reaction.

Conformational changes upon substrate binding enhance catalytic efficiency. Structural studies reveal conserved residues involved in coordinating zinc ions and substrate positioning.
