ENZYMEBASE
Hexokinase (EC 2.7.1.1)

Accepted Name

Hexokinase

Other Names

Glucokinase, D-hexokinase, glucose kinase, ATP:D-hexose 6-phosphotransferase, hexose kinase

Comments

Catalyzes the phosphorylation of glucose and other hexoses. It’s a key enzyme in glycolysis. Several isoenzymes exist in mammals with tissue-specific functions. The liver isoform (glucokinase) has lower affinity for glucose.

Function and Importance

Hexokinase initiates the glycolytic pathway by phosphorylating glucose to glucose-6-phosphate, which traps glucose inside the cell and marks it for metabolism. This step is crucial because it regulates the flow of glucose into energy-producing pathways and other biosynthetic routes.

  • Essential for cellular energy production.
  • Regulates glucose homeostasis.
  • Different isoforms adapted for tissues with varying glucose demands.
  • Glucokinase acts as a glucose sensor in pancreatic beta cells.

Structure Details

Hexokinase is a globular protein composed of two similar domains, each capable of binding glucose and ATP. The enzyme undergoes a conformational change upon substrate binding, which facilitates catalysis. The structure allows precise positioning of substrates to transfer the phosphate group efficiently.

The enzyme's active site contains amino acid residues that interact with glucose and ATP, stabilizing the transition state and lowering activation energy.
